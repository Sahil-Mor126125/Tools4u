How Browser-Based JPG, PNG & WebP to PDF Conversion Works

Whether you are combining smartphone photos of signed contracts, assembling expense receipts for accounting, or submitting scanned academic transcripts to a university portal, turning loose image files into a single multi-page PDF keeps your pages ordered and universally readable. Unlike traditional cloud converters that upload your private documents to external servers, SahilWebKit builds the entire PDF file directly inside your browser's local memory.

1. Zero-Upload Architecture: Why Local Conversion Matters

Most free online image-to-PDF tools transmit your files across the internet to a remote Linux server, process them in a queue, and store temporary copies on cloud disks. When your photos contain passports, driver's licenses, bank statements, or medical records, uploading them to unknown servers creates unnecessary privacy exposure.

SahilWebKit combines the native HTML5 Canvas API with the open-source pdf-lib binary PDF engine right inside your tab. Your photos are rotated, scaled, and assembled into raw PDF byte streams locally on your phone or laptop—meaning zero network upload delay and complete confidentiality.

2. Choosing A4 vs. US Letter vs. Fit-to-Image Page Size

Selecting the right page size ensures your PDF prints cleanly without awkward cropping or stretched borders:

  • Fit to Image (Default): Creates each PDF page at the exact pixel aspect ratio of that photo—ideal for digital viewing, screenshots, and mixed portrait/landscape phone snaps with zero white borders.
  • A4 (210 × 297 mm): The international standard paper size used across the UK, Europe, India, Australia, and global government/visa portals.
  • US Letter (8.5 × 11 in): Required for North American legal filings, IRS/tax forms, and US university applications.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Are my scanned IDs, receipts, or photos uploaded to a server?

No. Every image is read, rotated, compressed, and embedded into your PDF strictly inside your browser's local memory. Your documents never touch any server, and you can even disconnect from Wi-Fi after loading this page and convert files offline.

2. How many images can I combine into one PDF file?

There is no artificial page cap. Because conversion runs on your own device's RAM, you can combine 20 to 100+ images into one PDF. For large batches of high-megapixel phone photos, choose Balanced Web (85%) or Compact Email Size (65%) for fast generation.

3. How do I fix sideways or upside-down phone photos before exporting?

Each uploaded image card has a Rotate 90° button as well as Move Up and Move Down buttons. Rotate any sideways scan until the thumbnail preview looks upright, arrange the page sequence, and click Create PDF Now.

4. How are WebP images and transparent PNGs handled?

PDF documents natively support JPEG and PNG streams rather than WebP. SahilWebKit automatically converts WebP files and composites transparent PNG backgrounds onto crisp white paper so every page displays properly in Adobe Acrobat, Chrome, and mobile viewers.

5. How can I shrink a 10-photo PDF under 2 MB for email or government portals?

Smartphone cameras produce 4000×3000 px images that quickly create 20+ MB PDFs. Select Compact Email Size (65% JPEG) in the quality menu—SahilWebKit automatically scales oversized photos down to a sharp 1600 px document resolution and applies clean JPEG compression, routinely cutting total PDF file size by 80%–90% while keeping text clearly legible.
