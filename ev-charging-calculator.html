How to Calculate Real-World EV Home Charging Costs & Savings

Switching from an internal combustion vehicle to a battery electric vehicle (EV) shifts your transportation energy bill from unpredictable petrol stations to your home utility meter. However, accurate budgeting requires accounting for AC-to-DC charging losses, time-of-use electricity tariffs, and seasonal efficiency variations rather than multiplying raw battery kWh by daytime electricity rates.

1. Accounting for Wall-to-Battery Charging Efficiency

Every electric vehicle uses an onboard charger to convert alternating current (AC) from your wall box into direct current (DC) stored inside the lithium-ion battery pack. During Level 2 charging (240V at 7.4 kW to 11.5 kW), roughly 8% to 10% of incoming electricity is consumed by inverter conversion heat and battery cooling pumps. Level 1 trickle charging on a standard 120V/15A outlet experiences higher overhead—often 14% to 16%—because vehicle computers remain awake for 24+ hours. Our calculator automatically divides stored battery energy by 0.90 so your wall-meter cost reflects real utility billing.

3. Frequently Asked Questions

How much can an off-peak EV tariff cut charging bills?

Many US and UK utilities offer overnight EV rates between $0.07–$0.11/kWh (or £0.07–£0.09/kWh in the UK) between midnight and 5:00 AM. Scheduling your charger inside your car's app to run only during that window can cut annual electricity costs by more than 50%.

Should I charge my EV to 80% or 100% daily?

For NMC/NCA lithium-ion batteries, keeping daily charging capped at 80% minimizes cathode stress and extends pack longevity, saving 100% charges for long highway trips. LFP (Lithium Iron Phosphate) packs can be charged to 100% weekly for BMS calibration.
