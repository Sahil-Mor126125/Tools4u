Complete Guide to SaaS Churn, LTV, and CAC Payback Metrics

Building a sustainable Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) company depends on balancing recurring subscription revenue against upfront customer acquisition expenses. Unlike one-time retail sales, SaaS businesses spend capital today through paid search, outbound sales, and onboarding teams to earn predictable monthly recurring revenue (MRR) over multi-year customer lifespans. Tracking four core metrics—Customer Lifetime Value (LTV), Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), CAC Payback Period, and Net Dollar Retention (NDR)—reveals whether scaling marketing spend will compound enterprise value or drain cash reserves.

1. Why Gross Margin Must Be Included in SaaS LTV

A frequent modeling mistake is multiplying Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) by customer lifespan without factoring in Gross Margin. Cloud infrastructure, third-party API fees, payment processing charges, and customer support salaries represent real Cost of Goods Sold (COGS). The institutional formula used by venture capital analysts is:

Gross-Margin LTV = (Monthly ARPU × Gross Margin %) ÷ Monthly Logo Churn Rate

For example, if your product charges $150 per month with an 80% gross margin and experiences 3% monthly churn, your average customer stays 33.3 months and generates $4,000 in gross profit—not $5,000. Comparing that true gross-profit LTV against CAC prevents founders from overestimating marketing budgets.

3. Frequently Asked Questions

How does Net Dollar Retention (NDR) exceed 100%?

When monthly expansion revenue from seat additions, usage tiers, and add-ons exceeds the revenue lost from cancellations and downgrades, existing customer cohorts grow automatically every year even without new signups.

Why is CAC Payback Period critical for bootstrapped founders?

Even a SaaS with a 5:1 LTV:CAC ratio can run out of cash if CAC takes 24 months to recover. Keeping CAC payback under 9 months lets bootstrapped teams recycle subscription cash flow directly into new growth campaigns.
